FPGA Audio Processor

A synchronous digital signal processing pipeline built in VHDL — driving a hardware CODEC and filtering real‑time audio streams entirely in programmable logic.

Overview

This project was pre‑silicon validation work for digital signal processing: designing a synchronous architecture on an FPGA, timed to a 50 MHz master clock, that could take in live audio, filter it, and pass it back out — all in hardware, with no software in the loop.

What I built

  • A synchronous VHDL architecture aligned to a single 50 MHz master clock across the design.
  • Custom data‑forwarding protocols to initialise and drive an onboard hardware CODEC chip over I2C, and manage the real‑time audio stream once it was running.
  • A serial‑to‑parallel conversion interface feeding a parallel FIR filter, so incoming serial audio data could be processed through the filter chain in hardware.

Challenge

Everything downstream of the CODEC depended on getting the I2C initialisation and clocking exactly right — any timing mismatch between the serial input and the parallel filter stage would corrupt the signal rather than just producing a visible error. That pushed the design toward careful, deliberate synchronisation rather than trial and error.

Outcome

A working hardware audio pipeline — CODEC initialisation, real‑time streaming, and FIR filtering — running entirely on the FPGA. It's also where I got comfortable thinking in hardware‑software co‑design terms: deciding what belongs in programmable logic versus what belongs in software, before writing either.
